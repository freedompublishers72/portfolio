# Prompt Record — Upwork Phase 1.1B Economic Ranking

Verbatim prompt as provided, 2026-09-24:

---

You are executing Upwork Economic Viability Phase 1.1B — Economic Ranking.

INPUT

Use the complete evidence produced by Phase 1.1A.

OBJECTIVE

Sort the technical universe using measurable economic and structural characteristics so that the strongest areas can be selected for deeper investigation in Phase 1.1C.

This is a screening/ranking mechanism, not a final business conclusion.

CORE QUESTION

Which technical areas combine:

- real paid demand,
- sufficient project value,
- suitability for short contracts,
- manageable acquisition cost,
- tolerable competition,
- technical difficulty,
- evidence of constrained capable supply,
- recurring/persistent problems,
- and resistance to pure commodity execution?

METRICS

Calculate or estimate, where the evidence permits:

1. Demand frequency
2. Median project value
3. Budget distribution
4. Median proposal competition
5. Short-project frequency
6. Median/typical project duration
7. Connects required
8. Acquisition cost per proposal
9. Budget / Connect cost
10. Budget / competition
11. Competition-adjusted project value
12. Frequency of troubleshooting/repair work
13. Frequency of specialist work
14. Frequency of cross-system work
15. Frequency of recurring operational problems
16. Evidence of technical difficulty
17. Evidence of supply restriction
18. Commodity/AI-solvability
19. Frequency of jobs suitable for a one-person operation
20. Frequency of jobs suitable for completion within approximately one business week

Do not fabricate metrics where the sample does not support them.

RANKING MODEL

Create a transparent scoring/sorting framework.

Separate the dimensions into:

A. Economic attractiveness
B. Acquisition economics
C. Project structure
D. Technical difficulty / supply restriction
E. Commodity / AI resistance
F. Short-contract suitability
G. Recurring demand

Do not allow raw job volume to dominate the analysis.

Where data quality differs between categories, explicitly show confidence.

IMPORTANT

Do not simply reward expensive projects.

A $5,000 project lasting six months may be less relevant to this investigation than a $300 project that can realistically be diagnosed and completed in two days.

Likewise, a technically difficult market with 50+ proposals may not provide the hypothesized supply restriction.

OUTPUT

Produce:

1. Ranking methodology
2. Metric definitions
3. Evidence-quality rules
4. Comparative table of all materially investigated technical areas
5. Economic ranking/sorting
6. Top candidate areas for Phase 1.1C
7. Areas eliminated from further investigation and the factual reason
8. Areas requiring more evidence
9. Sensitivity analysis showing whether conclusions change under different weighting assumptions
10. Phase 1.1C investigation plan

Do not present the ranking as a business verdict.

The purpose is to identify where deeper evidence gathering is justified.
